About

OKZGN is the applied AI, platform and software engineering practice of Elías Francisco Alvarado Soshina. The name dates from 2019; the work behind it, from more than twenty years of designing and building software.

Table of Contents

    Professional Profile

    OKZGN is an independent practice that designs, builds and runs every layer of a digital product: AI agents and models, architecture and audits, web and mobile apps, infrastructure, and the DevOps and FinOps work that keeps them fast and affordable.

    Elías leads every engagement himself, from the first conversation to the handoff, and much of the tooling behind the work is open source. See the full catalog in the services section.

    The Name

    OKZGN comes from the word oxygen, reduced to the letters of its sound: O, K, Z, G, N. Like O2, it stands for what a system cannot run without and never notices while everything works.

    Tagline

    Less overhead. More intelligence.

    Overhead is what a system carries without needing it: dependencies, idle servers, manual steps, wasted tokens. OKZGN removes it and invests the savings in the part that thinks. The line extends the first tagline, Every byte matters, from code to whole systems.

    Core Values

    • Understand it to the core. A problem is broken down to its parts before anything is built. When no existing tool fit, Elías wrote his own: a CSS selector engine, a web server, a query language.
    • Lean by design. Few dependencies, and nothing to maintain that does not earn its place. Estructura and Componentes have no dependencies, the 2019 hosting platform needs no database, and this website has no build step.
    • Proven, not promised. Benchmarks, tests, fuzzing and security audits ship with the code, as in Turbo, the write-ahead-log ingestion engine and the benchmarks in the articles.
    • Secure by default. Least-privilege access, per-tenant sandboxing and crash-resilient storage are the starting point. Credentials are never shared over chat.
    • One craft, end to end. Design and code are one discipline, handled by the same engineer from start to finish. Every project ends in a documented handoff: tagged repositories, reproducible builds and architecture notes the client owns.

    Ideal Clients

    • Startups and product teams that need senior architecture without a full platform team: an MVP that has to scale, a first AI feature, a stack decision that will last.
    • Small and mid-sized businesses that want to automate operations with AI agents, move daily work to cloud tools such as point of sale or ticketing, and keep infrastructure costs under control.
    • Enterprises and engineering teams with specialized needs: high-throughput AI services, open-source models served on private GPUs, technical audits, DevSecOps and FinOps.
    • Agencies and software companies that need a technical partner behind their own brand: hosting, performance work and white-label platforms.
    • Organizations with large file archives that need to store, search and extract information at multi-terabyte scale.

    What they share: they want software they own, results they can measure, and systems that run without a heavy stack.

    Two Decades of Building

    Elías is self-taught. His path runs from graphic design to platform engineering and applied AI: a little over twenty years of building software that people use.

    The milestones below come from his archive of preserved and published projects, from his teenage work to the tools OKZGN uses today.

    1. 2005–2006

      Design first, then code. At 14 he teaches himself graphic design in Photoshop (icons, color palettes, small interface kits), then the HTML, CSS and JavaScript to make those designs work, and PHP.

    2. 2008

      A first client. Still a teenager, he writes a search engine that runs entirely in the browser, with ranking, "did you mean" suggestions and pagination. The same year he builds his first commercial system: the web platform of a karaoke software company, with a reseller network, credits, offline license activation and a searchable catalog of nearly 3,000 songs. He already signs his work, under the first of several names before OKZGN.

    3. 2010

      The client comes back. After a change of name, the same company returns, and he rebuilds the platform around a shared core, with AJAX, a reseller portal and white-label sites that distributors run on their own domains.

    4. 2011

      A system for a classroom. A university student in Quito commissions a learning platform for his class: students upload their assignments, the teacher grades them without reloading the page, and an administrator moderates a shared wall. Elías designs the whole visual identity himself.

    5. 2011–2013

      Writing his own tools. Instead of only using libraries, he builds them: Xleepr, a CSS selector engine for browsers that lacked one; Default 7, a framework for Windows desktop apps made with web technologies; and JustBikers, a cycling social network with its own window and dialog system and a media manager.

    6. 2014–2016

      Working with data. Alongside a steady stream of PHP websites, he writes scrapers that collect Bible texts from many sources and turns them into one consistent dataset.

    7. 2017

      From raw data to a product. That dataset becomes VB, a Bible reader and search tool that runs in the browser without a server and understands references such as "Gen 1:1-5; 2:1-3" across all 66 books.

    8. 2019

      The name OKZGN, and a hosting platform. After several earlier names, his practice becomes OKZGN. He builds a multi-tenant hosting platform with instant site provisioning, bandwidth and disk quotas, a built-in firewall and a control panel for every site, all without a database. He maintains it until 2023.

    9. 2020

      A query language of his own. A directory search tool lets users filter records with prefixes, ranges and regular expressions, then export the results to Excel.

    10. 2021–2023

      Retooling. He learns Go, Node, Angular, React and progressive web apps, writes Turbo, his own web server, and redesigns OKZGN's services from the ground up.

    11. 2024–2025

      Designing frameworks. Emerald, a type and DOM library he had refined since 2016, and Componentes, a component system with no build step, lead to Estructura, a framework built on multiple dispatch and published on npm in 2025.

    12. 2026

      Open source, and the archive. Turbo goes open source with tests, fuzzing and security audits; Estructura gains a family of extensions; the DX libraries reach npm; and the 2019 hosting platform is rewritten in Rust with per-tenant sandboxing. Applied AI becomes a core part of OKZGN's work, and his projects from 2008 onward are published, so the whole path can be read in code in the projects list.

    The names changed over the years; the principles in Core Values did not. Since 2019, the name is OKZGN.

    Visual Identity

    The basics of how OKZGN looks, so that every page, document and product reads as one brand.

    Logo

    The logotype is drawn on a geometric grid, not set in a typeface: one stroke width, equal letter widths, rounded outer corners and a G with its bar cut at a fixed angle. The O sets the proportions: its diameter is the cap height times the golden ratio, drawn as two concentric rings around an open center. On its own, the O is the symbol, used as the favicon and app icon.

    • OKZGN Blue on light backgrounds; white on dark ones. Never orange, gradients, shadows or outlines.
    • Clear space on every side at least as wide as one ring of the O.
    • Never stretched, rotated or retyped in a font.
    • Where the full logotype would be under 24 px tall, the symbol is used alone.

    Color Palette

    Neutrals carry most of the page, blue gives the identity, and orange marks only actions and live states: roughly 70, 20 and 10 percent.

    • OKZGN Blue#004E9BLogo, headings, links, primary buttons.
    • Bright Blue#0065CAActive and completed states.
    • Deep Blue#002447Base of dark-mode surfaces.
    • Sky#B8DBFFThe brand blue in dark mode.
    • Signal Orange#F26522Accent: calls to action, highlights, live states.
    • Burnt Orange#C6460COrange text and buttons on light backgrounds.
    • Ink#000D1AText in light mode, background in dark mode.
    • Paper#F4F4F5Background in light mode, text in dark mode.

    Typography

    AaNoto SansRegular and SemiBold. Headings, interface and body text.
    AaNoto Sans MonoRegular. Code, data, labels and figures.
    • Display and headings: SemiBold with tight tracking (−0.03 em on display titles, −0.01 em on headings). Display titles scale from 40 to 72 px, section titles from 30 to 44 px.
    • Body: Regular at 16 to 17 px, with a line height of 1.6, or 1.7 for long reading.
    • Labels: 13 px with slightly open tracking (+0.04 em).

    Design System

    • Tokens: every color, shadow, radius, size and duration is a named token; the light and dark themes are the same tokens with different values.
    • Surfaces: white cards on Paper in light mode, deep-blue cards on Ink in dark mode, with a 1 px hairline, a soft shadow and a faint top highlight. Blue-tinted and orange-tinted variants mark featured content.
    • Shape: corner radii on a fixed scale of 6, 8, 14, 20 and 28 px; buttons, tags and badges are pills.
    • Spacing: a 4 px base scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 px), content up to 1200 px wide, and reading columns of about 72 characters.
    • Icons: line icons with one stroke weight and round ends, in OKZGN Blue (Sky in dark mode). Small interface icons take the color of the text around them.
    • Motion: short transitions (about 0.2 s) with a slight spring on presses and checks. Every animation respects the reduced-motion setting.
