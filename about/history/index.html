Two Decades of Building

Born in Ukraine in 1991 and raised in Ecuador, Elías Francisco Alvarado Soshina started with drawing and painting and taught himself programming. This is his path to the practice he runs today, year by year.

Table of Contents

    His path runs from art and graphic design to platform engineering and applied AI: more than twenty years of building software that people use. The milestones below come from his own account and from his archive of preserved and published projects.

    Beginnings

    1. 1998–2002

      Drawing first, then code. At 7 he starts drawing and painting. At 10, a renowned painter from Zaruma teaches him to draw faces, trees and clouds, and to give forms light and shadow. The same year he discovers programming, and from then on he learns it on his own.

    2. 2005–2006

      A first job, and the web. Just before turning 14, he takes his first job, painting handmade candles in Carcelén, in the north of Quito, while already painting in oil at a professional level. At 14 he knows HTML, CSS and JavaScript well and has built many pages and small web applications with their own interface design, along with icons, color palettes and interface kits in Photoshop.

    First Platforms

    1. 2007–2008

      A first commercial platform. In 2007 he joins Cybermusic, a karaoke software company, in customer service and technical support, and at 16 he builds and launches its web platform: registration, user accounts, online license management with offline activation, a reseller network, credits and a searchable catalog of nearly 3,000 songs, written in PHP. In 2008 he also writes a search engine that runs entirely in the browser, with ranking, "did you mean" suggestions and pagination. He already signs his work, under the first of several names before the current one.

    2. 2010

      The company comes back. Renamed Karaoke Latinmusic, the company has him rebuild the platform around a shared core, with AJAX, a reseller portal and white-label sites that distributors run on their own domains.

    3. 2011

      A system for a classroom. A university student in Quito commissions a learning platform for his class: students upload their assignments, the teacher grades them without reloading the page, and an administrator moderates a shared wall. Elías designs the whole visual identity himself.

    4. 2011–2013

      Writing his own tools. Instead of only using libraries, he builds them: Xleepr, a CSS selector engine for browsers that lacked one; Default 7, a framework for Windows desktop apps made with web technologies; and JustBikers, a cycling social network with its own window and dialog system and a media manager.

    5. 2013

      A diploma, earned at night. While working, he graduates from the Miguel Sánchez Astudillo night technical high school in Zaruma, with a specialty in computer applications.

    Clients and Products

    1. 2014–2016

      Working with data. Alongside a steady stream of PHP websites, he writes scrapers that collect Bible texts from many sources and turns them into one consistent dataset.

    2. 2015–2018

      IT for five companies. He leads the IT of Cocarae, Petrounco, Cemplaza, Conbrics and Arnoba Print: he designs and runs their websites and cuts hosting costs by 80%, from $500 to $100 a year per site, with a lightweight PHP framework of his own.

    3. 2017

      From raw data to a product. That dataset becomes VB, a Bible reader and search tool that runs in the browser without a server and understands references such as "Gen 1:1-5; 2:1-3" across all 66 books.

    The practice

    1. 2019

      The name, and a hosting platform. After several earlier names, his practice takes the name it carries today, first seen as five variables in the code of the platform he builds that year: a multi-tenant hosting platform with instant site provisioning, bandwidth and disk quotas, a built-in firewall and a control panel for every site, all without a database. He maintains it until 2023.

    2. 2020

      A query language of his own. A directory search tool lets users filter records with prefixes, ranges and regular expressions, then export the results to Excel.

    3. 2021–2023

      Certified, then retooled. In January and February 2021, to certify years of self-taught experience and update the service catalog, he completes Platzi's Web Development and JavaScript schools, its learning paths in frontend with React.js, apps with React Native, development with Vue.js and backend with JavaScript, and courses in Node.js, Go and Angular. Over the next two years he writes Turbo, his own web server in Go, and redesigns the service catalog from the ground up.

    4. 2024

      Applied AI at scale. He joins VICDATA-EC as a senior software developer and builds a multi-tenant data analysis and messaging platform for more than 10,000 end users and 1,000 merchants, with AI-driven sales workflows on WhatsApp and Messenger. From then on, applied AI is the focus of his work, there and in his own practice: autonomous agents, inference pipelines and their costs.

    5. 2024–2025

      Designing frameworks. Emerald, a type and DOM library he had refined since 2016, and Componentes, a component system with no build step, lead to Estructura, a framework built on multiple dispatch and published on npm in 2025.

    6. 2026

      Open source, and the archive. Turbo goes open source with tests, fuzzing and security audits; Estructura gains a family of extensions; the DX libraries reach npm; and the 2019 hosting platform is rewritten in Rust with per-tenant sandboxing. His projects from 2008 onward are published, so the whole path can be read in code in the projects list.

    The names changed over the years; the principles in Core Values did not. Since 2019, the name has not changed.
